Project: CBSE Class 12 AI

Problem statement

Many people have limited awareness about diabetes and its risk factors. This project demonstrates how an AI-style classification system can use basic health and lifestyle features to provide an educational risk category.


Objectives

  • Spread diabetes awareness
  • Demonstrate AI concepts
  • Demonstrate classification
  • Demonstrate data processing
  • Promote responsible use of AI

AI domain

Prediction and classification using a rule-based (expert-system style) model on structured data. It shows how data becomes a decision, without claiming to be machine learning.

Input

Age group, height and weight (BMI), physical activity, family history, blood pressure, smoking, sugary food intake.

Processing

Validate inputs, compute BMI, assign points per feature, add the total.

Output

Lower, Moderate or Higher educational risk category with contributing factors.

Limitations

Simplified, not clinically validated, not a diagnostic tool.

Viva preparation

1. What is Artificial Intelligence?

Making machines do tasks that normally need human intelligence, like learning, reasoning and decision-making.

2. Why is this project related to AI?

It takes input data, analyses features and classifies the result, which is the basic prediction/classification workflow of AI.

3. What are the input features?

Age group, BMI, physical activity, family history, blood pressure, smoking and sugary food intake.

4. What is classification?

Putting data into predefined classes. Here: Lower, Moderate and Higher risk.

5. How does your risk assessment work?

Each answer gives points, the points are added, and the total is compared with thresholds to pick the category.

6. What is data preprocessing?

Cleaning and preparing data. We check valid ranges and convert height and weight to BMI and answers to numbers.

7. What is a false positive?

The system says higher risk but the person is actually not at risk.

8. What is a false negative?

The system says lower risk but the person actually is at risk. It can delay medical care.

9. Why can't this website diagnose diabetes?

Diagnosis needs blood tests by a doctor. We use a few self-reported answers and an unvalidated score.

10. What are the limitations of your model?

Few features, hand-made rules, no training on real data, self-reported inputs, possible bias.

11. How did you consider data privacy?

No name, phone or email is asked. Nothing is stored or sent; the calculation happens in the browser.

12. What is responsible AI?

Using AI fairly, safely and transparently, protecting privacy and keeping humans in control.

13. Is this machine learning?

No, it is rule-based. Machine learning would learn the rules from large datasets instead of us writing them.

14. How could you improve it?

Train a model on a proper public dataset, validate it with doctors and test it for fairness.
